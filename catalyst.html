Catalyst — an AI-powered employee growth platform for frontline industrial teams

A concept for connecting frontline operations with continuous employee development — turning plant context and safety requirements into a personalized daily workflow, while keeping managers in the decision loop.

01 — The Problem

Enterprise tools are scattered — operators pay the tax

While researching digital transformation in heavy manufacturing and energy, I noticed a recurring pattern: frontline engagement, continuous training, safety compliance, and day-to-day communication are usually spread across separate legacy tools. Operators spend time navigating systems instead of focusing on the work. Supervisors carry extra administrative load. Learning feels disconnected from the shift.

Catalyst is my exploration of what a single, context-aware workspace could look like for that operator — designed to demonstrate how I approach enterprise product problems: start from real operational friction, then design for clarity.

02 — The Concept

An AI co-pilot, not an AI replacement

The AI surfaces context and prioritizes work; it does not replace managerial judgment or run isolated evaluations. Tasks and learning modules adjust based on plant conditions, safety advisories, near-miss trends, and upcoming maintenance — so the system stays relevant to the shift instead of generic.

04 — UI Design

Designed for glanceability on the plant floor

The main workspace prioritizes execution: prioritized tasks, safety signals, personal progress, and quick access to procedures, with secondary navigation kept out of the way. High-contrast status, progressive disclosure, and consistent placement of safety-critical information were the working principles — patterns adapted from mature enterprise products like Microsoft Viva, Workday, and ServiceNow.

Frontline Operator Dashboard — AI task queue, performance score, rewards, safety reminder, weekly goals, achievements, and line leaderboard in one view.
AI-generated task queue — each row shows line, due time, estimate, and the reason it was surfaced (e.g. "flagged after last night's sensor drift alert").
Performance, safety & gamification sidebar — AI score, reward points, pinned near-miss alert, weekly goals, achievements, and leaderboard.
05 — System Architecture

Modular by role, not by cosmetic theme

To be useful at enterprise scale, the concept is modular — each role sees a deliberately reduced interface, not just a re-skinned dashboard.

  • Plant Operators — execution UI, task checklists, safety alerts, quick SOP access.
  • Shift Managers — exception monitoring, idea queue, line progress, shift handovers.
  • HR & L&D — skill heatmaps, certification status, engagement signals.
  • IT & Admin — connector health, permissions, AI guardrails and logs.
  • Leadership — multi-plant efficiency, innovation activity, safety summaries.

I also designed a low-friction innovation pipeline: an operator submits an idea in under a minute, AI checks for duplicates and generates a summary, and a manager approves, defers, or routes it in one step — keeping the approval decision human while reducing the noise that usually kills bottom-up ideas.

06 — AI-Assisted Workflow

How I actually used AI on this project

I used Claude as a working partner throughout — for structuring the research into a coherent problem statement, stress-testing the workflow logic (what should the AI decide versus what should stay with a manager), and drafting the executive proposal document alongside the Figma design work. The point wasn't to have AI generate the product — it was to move faster through explore → prototype → iterate without losing the reasoning behind each decision.

I deliberately kept the AI capability roadmap grounded rather than speculative: a RAG-style SOP assistant scoped to internal plant documents, predictive upskilling tied to upcoming equipment changes, and an automated shift-handover summary — capabilities that feel achievable in a real enterprise setting rather than a generic "AI does everything" pitch.

07 — What I Learned

Designing for a shift, not a screen

Catalyst pushed me past visual UI decisions into product logic: what an AI system should surface versus decide, how to keep safety information from getting buried under gamification, and how to design one interface that serves five very different jobs without forcing everyone through the same screens. It's the project that taught me the most about enterprise software specifically — legacy-tool fatigue, human-in-the-loop AI, and designing for glanceability under real operating conditions rather than a relaxed desktop session.

Catalyst is a portfolio demonstration of product thinking — research, interface design, and system structure — not a claim of a deployed product, real users, or measured outcomes.
