2D Platformer — animation, projectiles, and camera systems

A side-scrolling 2D platformer built with a partner under strict constraints (no advanced syntax, no modifying the provided skeleton code). It's the strongest evidence I have of interactive and animation thinking, not just static UI.

01 — My Role

What I personally built

In this duo project, I owned the character and enemy side of the game rather than the core engine scaffolding. Specifically:

  • Character and enemy animation — including the wizard enemy's cast/attack animation seen below
  • Multiple projectile types, including the green bolt sequences and laser attacks
  • Jump mechanics and platform collision feel
  • The horizontal scroll system that moves the camera with the player across each biome

The project's constraints meant working within a fixed skeleton and a limited syntax set — so the animation and enemy-behavior work had to be expressive within real technical limits, not built from scratch with unlimited tools.

02 — Screens

Two biomes, one enemy system reused across both

The game moves the player through a forest biome and a corrupted "hellscape" variant later in the level — sharing the same enemy (a spell-casting witch/wizard) and projectile logic, re-skinned for the different environment.

Forest biome — witch enemy mid-cast, projectile bolt sequence, coin collectibles, ladder traversal.
Hellscape variant — the same enemy type duplicated with a second attacker, plus a new dark "familiar" enemy and a cauldron hazard.
Early-level state — player and enemy at close range, showing the projectile telegraph before it reaches the player.
03 — Design Decisions

Interesting constraints, real decisions

Reusing one enemy type across two biomes (forest and hellscape) rather than designing entirely new enemies per zone kept the animation and projectile-collision work consistent, while re-skinning and adding a second simultaneous attacker in the later biome raised the difficulty without new systems to build from zero. The health bar, score counter, and coin pickups needed to stay legible against very different background palettes — a green/teal forest and a red/dark hellscape — which shaped how high-contrast the UI elements needed to be regardless of scene.
